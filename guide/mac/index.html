Say It for Mac
Installation & User Guide

Hold a key, speak, let go — your words appear wherever your cursor is. This guide covers everything from installing Say It to its most advanced features.

Version 1.0 macOS 26 · Apple Silicon Private by default

1What Say It is

Say It is a push-to-talk dictation app that lives in your Mac's menu bar. It turns speech into text in any app — Mail, Slack, Notes, a browser, a terminal — without switching windows.

🎙️

Push to talk

Hold Right ⌥, speak, release. The text is typed into the app you're using.

🔒

On-device

Transcription runs entirely on your Mac. Nothing you say is uploaded unless you turn on the optional AI features.

⚡

Fast

Text typically appears a fraction of a second after you let go of the key.

✍️

Clean text

Say “comma”, “period”, “new line”. Fillers like “um” and “uh” disappear automatically.

📖

Learns your words

A personal dictionary for names and jargon — and it can learn from your corrections.

✨

Optional AI

AI Polish tidies grammar to suit each app. Command mode rewrites selected text by voice.

3Installing Say It

Say It is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it installs like any app from a trusted developer — no security warnings to bypass.

  1. Download the installer.
    Go to github.com/bobrauck-ops/say-it-releases/releases/latest and click Say-It-<version>.dmg under Assets. It saves to your Downloads folder.
  2. Open the disk image.
    Double-click the .dmg file. A window opens showing Say It and a shortcut to your Applications folder.
  3. Drag Say It into Applications.
    Drag the Say It icon onto the Applications shortcut. If you're updating manually and macOS asks, choose Replace.
  4. Eject the disk image.
    In Finder's sidebar, click the eject button next to Say It, or drag the disk to the Trash. You can delete the .dmg from Downloads afterwards.
  5. Open Say It.
    Open it from Applications, Launchpad, or Spotlight (⌘ Space, type “Say It”, press Return). The first time, macOS may say it was downloaded from the internet — click Open.

Good to know

Say It has no Dock icon and no main window — it's a menu bar app. After launching, look for its icon near the clock at the top right of your screen (see The menu bar icon).

4First launch & setup

The first time Say It opens, a Welcome to Say It window walks you through four short steps. It takes about two minutes (plus the speech-model download).

Step 1 — Welcome

Shows your dictation key (Right ⌥ by default) and the basic idea: hold, speak, release. Click Get Started.

Step 2 — Setup

Three items, each turning into a green checkmark when ready. Continue unlocks once all three are done.

ItemWhy it's neededWhat to do
Microphone To hear you while you hold the key. Say It only listens while the key is held. Click Allow, then Allow in the macOS dialog.
Accessibility To notice the dictation key in other apps and to type the text for you. Click Open Settings. In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, switch on Say It (enter your Mac password if asked), then return to the welcome window. It notices automatically within a second.
Speech model The on-device engine that turns speech into text. Nothing — it downloads (~0.5 GB) and optimizes itself for your Mac in the background. Usually under a minute on a fast connection.

Why Accessibility?

macOS requires it for any app that watches for a key press while you're in another app, or that types text on your behalf. Say It uses it only for those two things, plus reading the text field after a dictation if learning from corrections is on.

Step 3 — Try it out

A practice area. Hold your dictation key and say the line shown:

Hello Say It comma this is my first dictation period

Release the key. Your words appear in the window — with “comma” and “period” turned into punctuation. The box outline lights up in the Say It colors while it's listening. Click Continue (or Skip).

Step 4 — You're all set

A cheat sheet of spoken punctuation. Click Start Dictating to close the window. You can reopen it any time from the menu bar icon → Welcome Guide….

6Dictating

The core of Say It: three motions, in any app.

  1. Click where you want the text — a message box, document, email, search field, terminal…
  2. Hold the dictation key (Right ⌥ — the Option key to the right of the space bar). You'll hear a soft click and the recording pill appears at the bottom of the screen.
  3. Speak naturally. Say punctuation where you want it (“comma”, “period”, “new line”). The pill's waveform moves with your voice.
  4. Release the key. A soft pop, the pill shows three dots for a moment, and your text is typed at the cursor.

How the text is inserted

Say It places the text on the clipboard, presses ⌘V for you, then restores whatever was on your clipboard before — so your clipboard is left as it was. If text was selected, the dictation replaces it, just as typing would.

Tips for the best accuracy

  • Start speaking just after pressing the key, and release just after your last word.
  • Speak at a normal pace and volume; there's no need to over-enunciate.
  • Use a built-in or wired microphone if you can. AirPods and other Bluetooth headsets drop to a lower-quality mode while recording.
  • Dictate in sentences or short paragraphs rather than single words — context helps the engine.
  • Add names and jargon to your personal dictionary.

Things that are ignored or cancelled

  • Very short taps (under a quarter of a second) are treated as accidental and discarded.
  • Pressing any other key while holding the dictation key cancels the recording — so using the key as part of a normal shortcut never starts a dictation.
  • Silence produces nothing; the pill says Didn't catch that.

Changing the dictation key

In Settings → General → Hold to dictate you can choose Right Option ⌥, Right Command ⌘, Right Control ⌃ or Fn / Globe 🌐. The left-hand versions of these keys keep working normally.

Using Fn / Globe

macOS also uses the 🌐 key for the emoji picker, switching input sources or its own dictation. To avoid both happening, open System Settings → Keyboard and set Press 🌐 key to → Do Nothing. Say It shows a reminder with a button to that setting if needed.

8Spoken punctuation & cleanup

The speech engines already add natural punctuation. On top of that, Say It understands spoken punctuation commands and removes filler words. This is the Clean up dictation setting (on by default).

Punctuation you can say

SayYou getExample
“period” or “full stop”.“It works period” → It works.
“comma”,“Hello comma Sam” → Hello, Sam
“question mark”?“Are you free question mark” → Are you free?
“exclamation point” or “exclamation mark”!“That's amazing exclamation point” → That's amazing!
“colon”:“Three things colon…” → Three things: …
“semicolon”;“First part semicolon second part” → First part; second part
“new line”a line break“Buy milk new line buy eggs” → two lines
“new paragraph”a blank line“Dear Sam comma new paragraph thanks…”

When the words stay words

Say It only converts these words when they're clearly commands:

  • After words like “the”, “a”, “this”, “that”, “my”, “our”… they stay as words: “the trial period ends Friday”, “a new line of products”, “the colon”.
  • “period” after common qualifiers stays a word: trial period, grace period, waiting period, notice period, billing period…
  • At the very start of a dictation, punctuation words stay as words (“Full stop at the end…”). “New line” and “new paragraph” still work there.
  • Numbers and web addresses such as 3.5 or example.com are never altered.

Automatic cleanup

  • Fillers removed: “um”, “uh”, “uhm”, “erm”, “hmm” — along with any commas around them. “I think, um, we should” → “I think we should”.
  • Doubled punctuation merged: if the engine already added a comma before your spoken “period”, you get just the period.
  • Capitalization: the first letter of the dictation, of each sentence, and of each new line is capitalized.

To get exactly what the engine heard with none of this processing, turn off Settings → Transcription → Clean up dictation.

9Speech engines

Say It includes two speech-to-text engines. Both run entirely on your Mac. Switch in Settings → Transcription → Engine.

Parakeet default

An open speech model from NVIDIA, running on your Mac's Neural Engine. The most accurate option for everyday English. Downloads once (~0.5 GB) on first use. Text appears in about a tenth of a second after release.

Apple

The speech engine built into macOS 26. No separate download beyond the language files macOS manages itself. Very fast; also benefits from your dictionary as vocabulary hints.

  • Switching engines takes a few seconds while the new one loads (the menu icon shows an hourglass). If you switch mid-dictation, the change applies once that dictation finishes.
  • Language: Parakeet is tuned for English. The Apple engine follows your Mac's language where macOS supports on-device recognition for it.
  • If one engine consistently mishears you, try the other — and add recurring problem words to your dictionary.

10Personal dictionary

Teach Say It how to write names, product names and jargon it gets wrong. Open Settings → Dictionary.

Adding words

  1. Click Add Word.
  2. In Write as, type the word exactly as it should appear — spelling and capitalization: Siobhan, iPhone, Kubernetes, Wispr Flow.
  3. Optionally, in Often heard as, type what the engine writes instead, separated by commas: shivon, chevonne.

Changes save automatically. Remove a word with the − button on its row.

Write asOften heard asEffect
Wispr Flowwhisper flow, whisper flo“whisper flow” → Wispr Flow
iPhone(empty)Fixes casing: “iphone” / “Iphone” → iPhone
Siobhanshivon, chevonne“Ask shivon” → Ask Siobhan

How matching works

  • Whole words only — “whisper flow” is replaced, “whispering flowers” is not.
  • Case doesn't matter when matching; your spelling and capitalization are always used in the result.
  • Longest match wins — “Wispr Flow Pro” beats “Wispr Flow”.
  • Commas and hyphens the engine inserts inside a phrase (“whisper, flow”) still match.
  • Your dictionary is applied last (after punctuation cleanup and after AI Polish), so your spellings always win.

Catch close mishearings

With this setting on (the default), Say It also fixes near-misses that sound like a dictionary word or one of its “often heard as” spellings — for example “Shivan” when you listed “shivon”.

Real words are never replaced

Near-miss matching only touches words that macOS's spell checker doesn't recognise. “Shaven” will never turn into “Siobhan”. The trade-off: macOS knows many first names, so a mishearing that happens to be a real name may not be caught automatically — add it under “Often heard as”.

Learning from your corrections

With Suggest words when I correct a dictation on (the default), Say It watches the text field for about a minute after each dictation. If you fix a word it typed, a card appears above the pill:

📖 Add “Siobhan” to your dictionary?Say It heard “Shivan” Not now Add
  • Add creates a dictionary entry, or adds the misheard spelling to an existing one. Not now dismisses it; it also fades after 10 seconds.
  • Clicking the card never takes focus away from the app you're typing in.
  • It's offered for capitalization fixes (iphone → iPhone) and fixes where either word isn't in the spell checker (Shivan → Siobhan). Swaps between two ordinary words (their → there) are treated as grammar and ignored.
  • It works in apps whose text macOS can read — Notes, TextEdit, Mail, Pages, Messages and most native apps. In many browsers and Electron apps (Slack, VS Code) it quietly does nothing.

11History

Every dictation is saved locally so you can find and re-copy it — handy if text went into the wrong window.

Quick access

Menu bar icon → Copy Recent lists your last five dictations. Click one to copy it.

The History window

Menu bar icon → History… (⌘Y while the menu is open).

  • Stats at the top: words today, words in total, and number of dictations.
  • Grouped by day — Today, Yesterday, then dates. Each entry shows the text, the time, the app it went into (with its icon), and the word count.
  • Search with the toolbar search field. It matches the text and the app name, every word must match, and accents don't matter: “budget mail” finds a Mail dictation about the budget; “cafe” finds “café”.
  • Copy — click the copy icon on a row, or double-click the row.
  • Delete one — right-click a row → Delete.
  • Delete everything — Clear All in the toolbar (asks for confirmation; can't be undone).

Storage & privacy

  • Keeps your latest 500 dictations; older ones drop off automatically.
  • Stored only on your Mac in ~/Library/Application Support/Say It/history.json. Never uploaded.
  • Practice dictations in the welcome window aren't saved.
  • Turn history off in Settings → General → Keep dictation history; clear it with Clear History….

12AI Polish optional

AI Polish sends each dictation to Anthropic's Claude to fix grammar, remove false starts, apply your self-corrections, and match the tone of the app you're in. It's off by default and needs your own Anthropic API key.

What it does

You sayYou get
“hey can you um send me the report by friday no wait thursday thanks” (in Mail)Hey, can you send me the report by Thursday? Thanks.
“for the trip we need tickets passports and a charger” (in Notes)For the trip, we need:
- Tickets
- Passports
- A charger
“git commit dash m fix the login bug” (in Terminal)git commit -m "fix the login bug"
“what time is the meeting tomorrow question mark”What time is the meeting tomorrow? (typed, not answered)

Setting it up

  1. Get an API key. Sign in at console.anthropic.com → API Keys → Create Key, and copy it (it starts with sk-ant-). API usage is billed by Anthropic separately from any Claude.ai subscription, so you may need to add a payment method or credits.
  2. Save it in Say It. Settings → AI Polish, paste the key, click Save. Say It runs a quick test — you should see a green Working — “…” line with a cleaned-up sample sentence.
  3. Turn it on. Switch on Polish dictation with Claude. (The switch stays locked until a key is saved.)

Styles

With Style → Match the app I'm typing in (the default), Say It picks a style from the app you're dictating into:

StyleUsed inBehaviour
EmailMail, Outlook, Spark, Superhuman, Mimestream, AirmailComplete, natural, professional sentences; new paragraphs where the topic changes.
ChatSlack, Messages, Discord, WhatsApp, Teams, Telegram, Messenger, Zoom, SignalCasual and concise; contractions kept; a single short sentence may drop its final period.
CodeTerminal, iTerm, Ghostty, Warp, Xcode, VS Code, Cursor, Windsurf, JetBrains IDEs, Zed, Sublime TextFile names, commands, flags and technical terms kept exactly; minimal edits; no added formatting.

Choose Light touch everywhere to use the Light style in every app.

Good to know

  • Dictations under three words (“Sounds good.”) are typed as spoken, without polish.
  • It adds about 0.5–1.5 seconds before the text appears; the pill shows dots meanwhile.
  • You never lose a dictation. If polish fails, takes longer than 6 seconds, is declined, or you're offline, the unpolished text is typed instead. If the problem is your API key, the pill tells you.
  • It edits, it doesn't answer. Questions and instructions in your dictation are typed as text — never answered or carried out.
  • Your dictionary still wins: your words are sent along as spelling hints and re-applied afterwards.
  • Cost: Say It uses Claude Haiku 5.5, Anthropic's fastest, lowest-cost model. A typical dictation costs a tiny fraction of a cent. Check Anthropic's pricing page for current rates; usage appears in your Anthropic Console.

Managing the key

Your key is stored in your macOS Keychain, not in a file. In Settings → AI Polish: Test re-checks it, Remove deletes it (and turns polish off). If macOS asks whether Say It may use the key from your Keychain, choose Always Allow.

13Command mode optional

Rewrite text by voice. Select some text, hold the command key, say what to do — Claude's result replaces your selection.

  1. Select text in any app.
  2. Hold Right ⌘ (the command key; configurable). The pill shows a ✦ sparkle next to the waveform so you know it's a command, not a dictation.
  3. Say an instruction, for example:
    • “make this friendlier” · “make this more formal” · “make this shorter”
    • “turn this into bullet points” · “fix the typos” · “summarize this in one sentence”
    • “translate to Spanish” · “rewrite this as a polite decline”
  4. Release. After a moment the selection is replaced with the result. Don't like it? Press ⌘Z in the app to undo.

Details

  • Requires an Anthropic API key — the same one as AI Polish (Settings → AI Polish). AI Polish itself doesn't need to be switched on.
  • What's sent: your selected text and your spoken instruction (transcribed on your Mac first). Nothing else.
  • Questions such as “what does this mean?” leave your text untouched, and the pill says No change — try rephrasing.
  • Limit: about 20,000 characters of selected text.
  • Timeout: 30 seconds. If it fails, your text is left exactly as it was.
  • Normal shortcuts still work. Holding Right ⌘ and pressing another key (⌘C, ⌘Tab…) cancels command mode.
  • Change or disable the key in Settings → General → Hold for command mode (Off, or any key other than your dictation key).

Known quirk in code editors

Some apps (browsers, Slack, VS Code) don't share their selection with macOS directly, so Say It copies it with ⌘C and then restores your clipboard. In code editors such as VS Code, ⌘C with nothing selected copies the current line — so a command with no selection acts on that line and inserts the result at the cursor. Press ⌘Z to undo, and select text first.

15Updates

  • Say It checks for updates automatically once a day. When one is available, a window shows what's new with options to install now, later, or skip that version.
  • Check any time: menu bar icon → Check for Updates….
  • Updates download, verify and install themselves, then relaunch Say It. Your settings, dictionary, history and API key are kept, and permissions carry over.
  • Every update is cryptographically signed; Say It refuses any update that isn't signed by its developer. Updates are also notarized by Apple.
  • Your current version is shown near the bottom of the menu.
  • Release notes for every version: github.com/bobrauck-ops/say-it-releases/releases.

16Privacy & your data

🔒 Stays on your Mac — always

Your voice and audio · transcription · spoken-punctuation cleanup · your dictionary · your history · your settings · your API key (in the Keychain).

☁️ Sent to Anthropic — only if you use it

AI Polish: the text of dictations of 3+ words, the app's name and your dictionary words.
Command mode: the selected text and your transcribed instruction.

  • Audio is never saved to disk and never leaves your Mac. Say It only listens while you hold a key.
  • Requests to Anthropic go directly from your Mac using your own API key, under Anthropic's API terms and privacy policy.
  • Update checks contact GitHub to download the update feed; they send no personal data.
  • Say It writes diagnostic logs to the macOS system log (subsystem com.robertrauck.sayit). The text of your dictations is redacted in these logs.

17Troubleshooting

Click a problem to see the fix. For anything not covered, the status line in the menu often explains what went wrong.

Getting started

I can't find the Say It icon in the menu bar

It's probably hidden behind the camera notch. Quit one or two other menu bar apps, or hold ⌘ and drag other icons left. Make sure Say It is running: open it again from Applications or Spotlight.

The welcome window's “Continue” stays disabled

All three items must show green checkmarks. For Accessibility, make sure Say It is switched on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. If the speech model is still downloading, wait — the spinner turns into a checkmark when done.

The speech model download is stuck or failed

Check your internet connection, then quit Say It (menu → Quit Say It) and reopen it — the download resumes. If problems persist, switch to the Apple engine in Settings → Transcription, which doesn't need the download.

Dictation

Holding the key does nothing
  • Check the menu status line. If it says “Preparing speech model…”, wait for it to finish.
  • Accessibility must be on — without it, Say It can't see the key in other apps. Check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.
  • Make sure you're holding the right-hand key you chose in Settings → General, not the left one.
  • Don't press other keys while holding it — that cancels the recording.
The pill appears, but no text is typed
  • If the pill says Copied — press ⌘V to paste, Accessibility is off: press ⌘V to paste, then re-enable Accessibility.
  • If it says Didn't catch that, check the microphone in Settings → Microphone and that the waveform moves when you speak.
  • Make sure your cursor is in a text field. If the welcome window is open on the “Try it out” step and in front, dictations go there instead.
  • A few apps block pasting (some password fields, secure terminals). Use Copy Recent and paste manually.
The waveform doesn't move

Say It isn't receiving audio. Check Settings → Microphone, make sure that mic isn't muted, and confirm Microphone access in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone.

Words are often wrong
  • Use a built-in or wired microphone instead of Bluetooth.
  • Try the other engine in Settings → Transcription.
  • Add recurring names and terms to the dictionary, including how they're misheard.
  • Start speaking just after pressing the key and release just after finishing.
“period” / “comma” are typed as words

Check that Settings → Transcription → Clean up dictation is on. Also note the deliberate exceptions in Spoken punctuation — e.g. after “the” or at the very start of a dictation.

Fn / Globe opens the emoji picker as well

Set System Settings → Keyboard → Press 🌐 key to → Do Nothing.

Using Right ⌘ for shortcuts starts a dictation or command

It shouldn't — pressing any other key while holding cancels. If you hold Right ⌘ alone for a moment before deciding on a shortcut, a recording may start and then cancel when you press the second key. Choose a different key in Settings → General if this bothers you.

AI Polish & command mode

I can't turn on AI Polish

The switch unlocks after you save an API key in the same pane. Paste it, click Save, and wait for the green Working line.

Testing the API key shows a red error
  • “key was rejected” — the key is wrong or was revoked; copy it again from the Anthropic Console (it starts with sk-ant-).
  • “isn't allowed to use…” — your Anthropic account or key doesn't have access; check the Console.
  • “rate limit reached” / “busy” — temporary; try again shortly. Also check that your account has credits.
  • “Couldn't reach Anthropic” — check your internet connection, VPN or firewall.
macOS asks if Say It may use a Keychain item

That's your API key. Enter your Mac login password if asked and choose Always Allow. This can happen once after updates.

Polish made a change I didn't want

Switch Style to Light touch everywhere for minimal edits, or turn polish off. Each dictation's final text is in History.

Command mode says “Select some text first” but I did

The app may not share its selection. Say It tries copying with ⌘C as a fallback; some apps block that too. Try selecting again, or copy the text into Notes or TextEdit and run the command there.

Permissions & updates

Permissions are switched on but don't seem to work

In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility (or Microphone), select Say It, click − to remove it, then reopen Say It and grant access again. This clears a stale entry left by an older copy of the app.

Launch at login doesn't work

Check System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions and make sure Say It is allowed. Say It shows a button to that page if approval is pending.

“Check for Updates…” fails

Check your internet connection and try again later. You can always download the latest version manually from the releases page and replace the app in Applications.

Collecting diagnostics

To see what Say It did during recent dictations, open the Terminal app and run:

/usr/bin/log show --last 10m --predicate 'subsystem == "com.robertrauck.sayit"' --info --style compact

The log shows each step — key pressed, text length, whether it was pasted or copied, polish timing and fallbacks — with the text of your dictations redacted. Use the full /usr/bin/log path; in some shells, plain log is a different command.

18Uninstalling

  1. Menu bar icon → Quit Say It.
  2. If you turned on launch at login, turn it off first in Settings → General (or remove Say It from System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions).
  3. Drag /Applications/Say It.app to the Trash.
  4. Optionally remove your data in Finder (Go → Go to Folder…, ⇧⌘G):
    • ~/Library/Application Support/Say It — dictionary and history
    • ~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio — the speech model (~0.5 GB)
  5. Optionally remove your settings and API key in Terminal: 
    defaults delete com.robertrauck.sayit
security delete-generic-password -s com.sayit.SayIt.anthropic
  6. Optionally remove Say It from System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and → Accessibility (select it, click −).

20FAQ

Does Say It work offline?

Yes. After the one-time model download, dictation, cleanup, dictionary and history all work without internet. AI Polish and command mode need a connection; if you're offline, polish is skipped and your text is typed as spoken.

Is Say It always listening?

No. It only records while you hold the dictation or command key. The macOS microphone indicator in the menu bar shows exactly when.

Do I need an Anthropic account?

Only for the optional AI Polish and command mode. Everything else is free and works without any account.

Which languages are supported?

Say It is designed for English. The Apple engine can follow other languages macOS supports for on-device recognition; spoken punctuation commands are English only.

Will it work in my browser / Slack / Terminal?

Yes — dictation works anywhere you can type and paste. Learning from corrections works only in apps whose text macOS can read; command mode works almost everywhere via the copy fallback.

Can I use Say It on an Intel Mac or an older macOS?

No. It requires Apple Silicon and macOS 26 or later.

How do I move my dictionary to another Mac?

Quit Say It on both Macs and copy ~/Library/Application Support/Say It/dictionary.json across. Your API key isn't included — add it again in Settings.
