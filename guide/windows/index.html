Say It for Windows
Installation & User Guide

Hold a key, speak, let go — your words appear wherever your cursor is. This guide covers everything from installing Say It to its most advanced features.

Version 1.0 Windows 11 · x64 Private by default

1What Say It is

Say It is a push-to-talk dictation app that lives in the Windows system tray. It turns speech into text in any app — Outlook, Teams, Word, a browser, a terminal — without switching windows.

🎙️

Push to talk

Hold Right Ctrl, speak, let go. The text is typed into the app you're using.

🔒

On-device

Transcription runs entirely on your PC. Nothing you say is uploaded unless you turn on the optional AI features.

⚡

Fast

Text typically appears about a second after you let go of the key.

✍️

Clean text

Say “comma”, “period”, “new line”. Fillers like “um” and “uh” disappear automatically.

📖

Learns your words

A personal dictionary for names and jargon — and it can learn from your corrections.

✨

Optional AI

AI Polish tidies grammar to suit each app. Command mode rewrites selected text by voice.

3Installing Say It

Say It installs just for your Windows account — no administrator password needed — and keeps itself up to date afterwards.

  1. Download the installer.
    Download SayIt-win-Setup.exe. It's always the newest version and saves to your Downloads folder. (Every version is also listed on the releases page.)
  2. Run it.
    Open the downloaded file. If Windows shows Windows protected your PC, click More info, then Run anyway (see the note below).
  3. That's it.
    Setup takes a few seconds, adds Say It to the Start menu, and starts it. The welcome guide opens.

About the “Windows protected your PC” message

Windows shows this for apps from publishers it doesn't know yet. Say It isn't code-signed, so Windows can't show a verified publisher name; More info → Run anyway installs it normally. On work or school PCs managed by an IT department, policies may block unsigned apps completely — the installer or Say It itself then won't open (“Access is denied” or “This app has been blocked”). Ask your IT department in that case.

Good to know

Say It has no taskbar button and no main window — it's a tray app. Look for its icon near the clock; Windows often tucks new icons under the ^ arrow (see The tray icon).

4First launch & setup

The first time Say It starts, a Welcome to Say It window walks you through four short steps. It takes about two minutes, plus the speech-model download.

  1. Welcome. A quick look at how it works: hold Right Ctrl, speak, let go. Click Get started.
  2. Getting ready. Say It downloads its speech model, NVIDIA Parakeet (about 480 MB, once), and checks your microphone. Each item shows a checkmark when it's ready; Continue becomes available once the model has loaded.
    • If the microphone row says access is turned off, click Open privacy settings and switch on Microphone access and Let desktop apps access your microphone.
    • If it says no microphone was found, plug one in or check Settings → System → Sound.
  3. Try it. Click in the practice box, hold Right Ctrl, say “Hello Say It comma this is my first dictation period”, and let go. Your sentence appears in the box, with “comma” and “period” turned into punctuation.
  4. You're all set. Click Start dictating.

You can open the guide again any time from the tray menu: Welcome guide….

5The tray icon

Say It lives in the system tray at the bottom right of the screen. If you don't see the icon, click the ^ arrow next to the clock. To keep it always visible, drag it from there onto the taskbar, or turn it on in Settings → Personalization → Taskbar → Other system tray icons.

Hover over the icon to see what Say It is doing (for example Ready: hold Right Ctrl and speak or Downloading speech model… 42%). Double-click it to open Settings. Right-click it for the menu:

6Dictating

Click where you want the text — an email, a chat box, a document — then:

  1. Hold Right Ctrl. A soft rising chime plays and the pill appears with a live waveform.
  2. Speak naturally. Keep holding the key the whole time.
  3. Let go. A falling chime plays, the pill shows pulsing dots, and about a second later your words are typed where your cursor is, followed by a space so you can keep going.

Good to know

  • Cancel: while holding the key, press any other key. Nothing is typed. This also means shortcuts such as Right Ctrl+C keep working normally.
  • Quick taps are ignored: holding for less than a quarter of a second doesn't start a dictation.
  • Your clipboard is preserved. Say It types by pasting, then puts back the text you had copied before.
  • Nowhere to type? If the desktop or taskbar has focus, the text is left on the clipboard and the pill says Copied — press Ctrl+V to paste.
  • Apps running as administrator: Windows doesn't let normal apps type into programs started with “Run as administrator”. Dictation won't appear there.
  • Long dictations are fine — a minute or more works; it just takes a little longer to transcribe.
  • A different key: choose Right Alt or Right Shift in Settings → General. Right Ctrl is the default because Right Alt is AltGr on many keyboard layouts, used for accents and symbols.

8Spoken punctuation & cleanup

The speech engine already adds natural punctuation. On top of that, Say It understands spoken punctuation commands and removes filler words. This is the Clean up dictation setting (on by default).

Punctuation you can say

SayYou getExample
“period” or “full stop”.“It works period” → It works.
“comma”,“Hello comma Sam” → Hello, Sam
“question mark”?“Are you free question mark” → Are you free?
“exclamation point” or “exclamation mark”!“That's amazing exclamation point” → That's amazing!
“colon”:“Three things colon…” → Three things: …
“semicolon”;“First part semicolon second part” → First part; second part
“new line”a line break“Buy milk new line buy eggs” → two lines
“new paragraph”a blank line“Dear Sam comma new paragraph thanks…”

When the words stay words

  • After words like “the”, “a”, “this”, “that”, “my”, “our”… they stay as words: “the trial period ends Friday”, “a new line of products”.
  • “period” after common qualifiers stays a word: trial period, grace period, notice period, billing period…
  • At the very start of a dictation, punctuation words stay as words. “New line” and “new paragraph” still work there.
  • Numbers and web addresses such as 3.5 or example.com are never altered.

Automatic cleanup

  • Fillers removed: “um”, “uh”, “uhm”, “erm”, “hmm” — along with any commas around them.
  • Doubled punctuation merged: if the engine already added a comma before your spoken “period”, you get just the period.
  • Capitalization: the first letter of the dictation, of each sentence, and of each new line is capitalized.

To get exactly what the engine heard, turn off Settings → Transcription → Clean up dictation.

9Personal dictionary

Teach Say It how to write names, product names and jargon it gets wrong. Open Settings → Dictionary.

Adding words

  1. Click Add word.
  2. In Write as, type the word exactly as it should appear: Siobhan, iPhone, Kubernetes.
  3. Optionally, in Often heard as, type what Say It writes instead, separated by commas: Shivan, Chevonne.

Changes save as soon as you click out of a box. Remove a word with the bin button on its row.

How matching works

  • Whole words only — “whisper flow” is replaced, “whispering flowers” is not.
  • Case doesn't matter when matching; your spelling and capitalization are always used.
  • Longest match wins, and your dictionary is applied last (after cleanup and after AI Polish), so your spellings always win.

Catch close mishearings

With this setting on (the default), Say It also fixes near-misses that sound like a dictionary word, such as “Chivon” when you listed “Siobhan”.

Real words are never replaced

Near-miss matching only touches words that the Windows spell checker doesn't recognise. The trade-off: Windows knows many first names, so a mishearing that happens to be a real name may not be caught — add it under “Often heard as”.

Learning from your corrections

With Suggest words when I correct a dictation on (the default), Say It watches the text field for a minute after each dictation. If you fix a word it typed and pause, a card appears above the pill:

📖 Add “Siobhan” to your dictionary?Say It heard “Shivan” Not now Add
  • Add creates a dictionary entry, or adds the misheard spelling to an existing one. Not now dismisses it; it also fades after 10 seconds.
  • Clicking the card never takes focus away from the app you're typing in.
  • It's offered for capitalization fixes (iphone → iPhone) and fixes where either word isn't in the spell checker (Shivan → Siobhan). Swaps between two ordinary words (their → there) count as grammar and are ignored.
  • It only reads the field — it never selects, copies or types anything. It works in apps that share their text with Windows accessibility (Notepad, Word, Outlook, most browser text boxes); elsewhere it quietly does nothing.

10History

Every dictation is saved on your PC so you can find and re-copy it — handy if text went into the wrong window. Open it from the tray menu: History….

  • At the top: words today, words total and the number of dictations.
  • Dictations are grouped by day, newest first, with the time, the app and the word count.
  • Search matches the text and the app name, ignoring case and accents.
  • The copy button puts a dictation back on the clipboard; the bin button deletes it. Clear all deletes everything (after asking).
  • The last 500 dictations are kept. Command-mode results are saved too.
  • Turn history off in Settings → General → Keep dictation history. It never leaves your PC.

11AI Polish

AI Polish sends each dictation to Claude (Anthropic's AI) to fix grammar, remove false starts and apply self-corrections — “meet Tuesday, no wait, Wednesday” becomes “meet Wednesday”. It's off until you add an API key.

Turning it on

  1. Get an API key: sign in at console.anthropic.com, open API keys, click Create key, and copy it (it starts with sk-ant- and is only shown once).
  2. In Say It, open Settings → AI Polish, paste the key and click Save. Say It tests it straight away and shows ✓ Working — … with a sample.
  3. Polish switches on. Use the switch to turn it off and on later.

Style

SettingWhat happens
Match the app I'm typing in (default)Email apps (Outlook, Thunderbird) get complete, professional sentences; chat apps (Teams, Slack, Discord, WhatsApp, Zoom) stay casual; terminals and code editors (Windows Terminal, PowerShell, VS Code, Cursor, JetBrains IDEs) keep technical terms exactly as spoken; Word, Notepad, OneNote, Notion and Obsidian get clear prose, with spoken lists formatted as lists. Everything else gets a light touch.
Light touch everywhereOnly grammar, punctuation and false starts are fixed. Your wording stays the same.

Details

  • Dictations shorter than three words aren't polished.
  • If Claude doesn't answer within six seconds, or its answer looks wrong, your text is typed exactly as dictated — polish never loses what you said.
  • Your dictionary words are sent along so Claude spells them your way, and they're applied again afterwards.
  • Polish uses Claude Haiku 5.5 and typically adds about a second. Anthropic bills your API account for usage, which for dictation is a small amount.
  • Your key is stored in Windows Credential Manager (as SayIt.anthropic), never in a file.

12Command mode

Edit text by voice. Select some text, hold the command key (Right Alt by default), say what to do, and let go. Claude's rewrite replaces your selection. Needs an API key (see AI Polish).

  1. Select the text in any app.
  2. Hold Right Alt. The pill shows a sparkle ✦ next to the waveform.
  3. Say an instruction, then let go.

Things to try

  • “Make this more formal” · “Make this friendlier” · “Shorten this”
  • “Fix the grammar” · “Turn this into bullet points” · “Translate to French”
  • “Write this as a polite reply declining the meeting”

Details

  • Say It reads the selection while you speak. In apps that don't share it with Windows accessibility, it copies the selection after you let go and restores your clipboard.
  • Up to about 20,000 characters can be selected. The result may take a few seconds for long text (the limit is 30 seconds).
  • If something goes wrong, your text is left exactly as it was.
  • Change or turn off the command key in Settings → General → Hold for command mode. On keyboards where Right Alt is AltGr, choose Right Shift or turn it off.
  • Releasing Alt on its own normally opens an app's menu bar; Say It prevents that so the result isn't lost.

14Updates

Say It keeps itself up to date. About 30 seconds after it starts, and every six hours after that, it checks for a new version and downloads it in the background.

  • A downloaded update installs the next time Say It starts — you're never interrupted mid-dictation.
  • To install straight away, choose Restart to update in the tray menu (a notification tells you when one is ready).
  • Check for updates… in the tray menu checks immediately.
  • Your settings, dictionary, history and speech model are kept across updates.

15Privacy & your data

Say It only listens while you hold a key. Speech is transcribed on your PC; with AI Polish and command mode off, nothing you say ever leaves it.

  • AI Polish sends the transcribed text (not audio) of dictations of three words or more to Anthropic's API, along with the app's name and your dictionary words.
  • Command mode sends your selected text and spoken instruction (as text) to Anthropic's API.
  • The log records what Say It did (for troubleshooting), never what you said.

16Troubleshooting

Click a problem to see the fix. Hovering over the tray icon often explains what's going on, and Open log folder in the tray menu leads to say-it.log, which records every step (never your words).

Installing & starting

“Windows protected your PC” when running the installer

Click More info, then Run anyway. Windows shows this for apps from publishers it doesn't recognise yet.

“Access is denied” or “This app has been blocked” (work PCs)

Your organisation's security policy blocks apps that aren't on its approved list. Only your IT department can allow Say It.

I can't find the tray icon

Click the ^ arrow next to the clock. If it isn't there either, start Say It from the Start menu. Starting it a second time shows a message if it's already running.

The speech model download is stuck or failed

Check your internet connection, then quit Say It (tray → Quit Say It) and start it again; the download starts over. Company networks that block GitHub downloads can stop it — the model comes from github.com.

Dictation

Holding the key does nothing
  • Hover over the tray icon. If it says it's downloading or loading the model, wait.
  • Check the key in Settings → General.
  • Some keyboards (especially compact laptops) have no right Ctrl: choose Right Alt or Right Shift.
  • Remote desktop and virtual machine windows may capture the key before Say It sees it.
The pill appears but nothing is typed
  • If the app was started as administrator, Windows blocks typing into it — run it normally.
  • If the pill says Copied — press Ctrl+V to paste, the text is on your clipboard.
  • Some games and remote-desktop clients ignore pasted text.
“Didn't catch that” every time

Check Settings → Microphone (and that the right mic is the Windows default), and that Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone → Let desktop apps access your microphone is on. Speak after the start chime and keep holding the key until you finish.

Words are often wrong

Use a wired or built-in mic rather than Bluetooth, speak at a normal pace, and add names and jargon to your dictionary. AI Polish also fixes many small mistakes.

No start and stop sounds

Check Settings → General → Play start and stop sounds, and that Say It isn't muted in Settings → System → Sound → Volume mixer (it can appear as “Say It” or “SayIt”).

AI features

The key test says the key was rejected

Copy the key again from the Anthropic Console, click Remove, paste it and Save. Make sure the key hasn't been disabled and that your Anthropic account has credit.

Polish seems to do nothing

Dictations under three words aren't polished, and already-clean text comes back unchanged. If Claude takes longer than six seconds, your text is typed unpolished — check your connection.

Command mode says “Select some text first” although text is selected

A few apps share neither their selection nor a normal copy. Try selecting again and holding the key a moment longer, or copy and paste the text into an app such as Notepad or Word.

The “Add to your dictionary?” card never appears
  • Check Settings → Dictionary → Suggest words when I correct a dictation.
  • Pause briefly after your fix — the card only appears once you stop typing — and stay in the same window.
  • Swaps between two ordinary words (Sean → Shaun) are ignored by design.
  • The app must share its text with Windows accessibility; lines starting with “Correction watcher:” in the log say exactly what happened.

17Uninstalling

  1. Open Settings → Apps → Installed apps, find Say It, click … and choose Uninstall. This removes the app, its Start menu shortcut and the sign-in entry.
  2. Optional — to remove your data too, delete the folders %APPDATA%\Say It and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Say It, and remove SayIt.anthropic in Credential Manager → Windows Credentials.

19FAQ

Does Say It work offline?

Yes. After the one-time model download, dictation, cleanup, dictionary and history all work without internet. AI Polish and command mode need a connection; offline, polish is skipped and your text is typed as spoken.

Is Say It always listening?

No. It only records while you hold the dictation or command key. Windows shows a microphone icon in the taskbar exactly while it's recording.

Do I need an Anthropic account?

Only for the optional AI Polish and command mode. Everything else is free and works without any account.

Which languages are supported?

Say It is designed for English. Spoken punctuation commands are English only.

Will it work in my browser / Teams / Terminal?

Yes — dictation works anywhere you can type and paste (except apps running as administrator). Learning from corrections works in apps that share their text with Windows accessibility.

Is there a Mac version?

Yes, with the same features. See the Mac guide.

How do I move my dictionary to another PC?

Quit Say It on both PCs and copy %APPDATA%\Say It\dictionary.json across. Your API key isn't included — add it again in Settings.
